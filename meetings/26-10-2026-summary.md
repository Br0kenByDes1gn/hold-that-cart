# Meeting summary — "Hold That Cart" (26 Oct 2026)

**Participants:** Zaro, schmithvillers
**Context:** Meta global.ai hackathon, ~10 days, 2-person team. Goal is a convincing demo, not a production product.

## Product concept (agreed)

- The app is called **Hold That Cart**.
- Core loop: capture what you buy → build up a household inventory → use that inventory to give better buying suggestions the next time you shop.
- Suggestions cover three angles: **save money, healthier choice, lower carbon footprint / more sustainable**.
- Suggestions can go both ways: "you already have this, don't buy it" as well as "buy this instead".

## The problem that shaped the flow (agreed)

- A receipt is only available **at the end** of shopping, so receipt-based suggestions can only help the **next** trip — by which time the user may have forgotten them.
- Therefore the app needs suggestions at **three moments**, not just after checkout:
  1. **Before the trip** (from a shopping list)
  2. **During the trip** (from photos of the cart)
  3. **After the trip** (from the receipt → inventory)

## Inputs and what each one gives (agreed)

| Input | When | What it produces |
|---|---|---|
| **Shopping list** (photo of paper list, screenshot, typed, etc.) | Before / at start of trip | Suggestions for the **current** trip. New users with no history can only get **type-level** suggestions ("buy this type of oil"), not brand-level, since the app doesn't know local availability yet. |
| **Cart photo** | During the trip | Identifies visible items, gives alternatives, and can tick items off the list for the current trip. Only top-layer items are realistically identifiable. |
| **Receipt** | End of trip (or later) | The "solid evidence": brands, quantities, reliable item list. Fills the inventory and drives **next-trip** suggestions, including brand-level swaps. |

- Agreed to accept receipts from **both** Shopping Mode and Pantry Mode, since sometimes you scan immediately and sometimes you stuff the receipt in your pocket and scan later.
- Agreed that for the prototype it is fine not to know prices, exact availability, or perfect sustainability data. Brand and price intelligence would come later from a supermarket partnership.

## App structure and flow (agreed, and drawn in Excalidraw)

```
Start screen
 ├─ [Shopping Mode] ──────────────► Scanner
 └─ [Pantry Mode] ────────────────► Inventory
                                      │
Scanner ──(detected receipt)────────► Inventory
Inventory ──[Add receipt]───────────► Scanner (same reusable screen)
Inventory item ─────────────────────► Suggestion detail
```

- **Start screen:** two big buttons only — **Shopping Mode** and **Pantry Mode**. No complicated onboarding or step-by-step wizard.
- **Pantry Mode → Inventory:** shows what you have, historical purchases, next-trip suggestions, a **"Add receipt"** button, and is the place to create/pre-populate a new shopping list.
- **Naming:** "inventory" was judged too technical; **"pantry"** was chosen as the working name. "Fridge" was rejected because it implies only refrigerated items.
- **Scanner:** one **shared, reusable smart-scanner screen** used from both modes and for "add receipt". It auto-detects what it is looking at — receipt, grocery list, or cart/groceries — and proposes the relevant action. It shows the camera plus a **list of actions**, each as "current item → suggestion".
- **Suggestions UI:** compact, colourful **badges/tags** on items (e.g. "saves money", "healthy", "sustainable"), not dense text. Tapping the item/badge opens a detail view explaining the reasoning and the saving. Detail view was sketched as a popup/panel with the three tag types colour-coded.
- **Shopping list:** acts as a checklist with checkboxes, can be created from the pantry side (based on what you have/history) or from the shopping side.
- **Flexibility:** the same screens are deliberately reused and mixed rather than enforcing a strict linear path; the receipt scanner in particular is reachable from several places.

## UX principle (agreed)

- The app should feel closer to a **game than a traditional form-based app**: things happen immediately on tap, few choices, no pre-filling/validation steps, minimal explicit navigation. The intention is "simple and catchy" over "technically advanced".
- Explicitly not "gamification" with points/badges — just a fast, low-friction, immediate-feedback experience.

## Platform (agreed)

- Build a **PWA / web app**, not a native mobile app.
- Reasons: simpler development and testing, no separate iOS/Android builds, easy install prompt, works on both iPhone and Android, no store developer fees for the prototype (App Store ~$100/yr, Play ~$25/yr).
- If the idea gains traction, it can later be packaged with something like Capacitor or rewritten as a native app.

## Scope decisions (agreed)

- Optimise for a **demo/prototype**, not a sellable product.
- Don't sink time into hard accuracy problems (brand recognition, store availability, detailed sustainability indices such as egg codes).
- Focus effort on the flow, the experience, and the visual presentation.
- The Excalidraw schematic was explicitly framed as the **basis for AI-assisted design work**: export it, and an agent will turn it into a cleaner diagram/design.

## Ideas discussed but not agreed / left open

- **Phone mounted on the cart, continuously recording and recognising every few seconds.** Very slick demo (the list ticks itself off live), but no natural mounting point on a supermarket cart, phone theft/fall risk, battery drain, and heavy AI/compute cost. Treated as a possible "wow" demo gimmick, not a committed feature.
- **Scanning every individual item / barcode.** More precise identification, but far too much manual work and data; not adopted. A narrower fallback survived: if an item is recognised but the brand is unclear, ask the user to scan its barcode.
- **Taking a photo after each layer of the cart is filled.** Proposed as a workaround for occlusion; Zaro was non-committal ("we need to start somewhere"). Not decided.
- **Voice-dictated shopping list** ("press record and speak your list"). Zaro suggested it; depends on getting speech recognition from the Meta models. Not confirmed.
- **How strict the flow should be.** Zaro pushed for minimal structure and letting the smart scanner decide; schmithvillers still drew explicit paths per mode. Broad alignment, but the exact balance is unresolved.
- **Suggestion scoring.** Percentages / "how much money you save" / health scores were floated, then reduced to simple badges in the UI. How those values are actually computed and sourced is undefined.
- **Inventory data model.** The need for both a total inventory and per-purchase history (to predict consumption, e.g. "buys oil weekly") was agreed conceptually, but the model and the prediction logic were explicitly left for later.
- **Whether "pantry" is the final name.** Used consistently in the diagram, but the naming discussion ended tentatively.
- **Partnership with a supermarket chain** to get real product catalogues/availability and make suggestions dramatically better — flagged as the real long-term unlock, far out of scope for the hackathon.
- **Deep sustainability data sources** (e.g. egg farming codes) — acknowledged as meaningful but deliberately deprioritised.
- **Technical architecture and stack** beyond "PWA" — deferred until Meta's model access is known.
